# SailWindow Morning Brief — Monday, Nov. 2, 2026

## Coffee-check

Galveston is a light-air, post-fog day. The central Gulf has usable breeze but shower timing matters. Offshore Louisiana and exposed South Florida are the hard passes: easterlies, 3–6 ft seas, and thunderstorms make “windy” very different from “good sailing.” The NHC expects no tropical development in the Atlantic/Gulf during the next seven days.

## Sailing outlook and windows

- **Upper Texas / Galveston Bay:** SE 5–10 kt, smooth water, patchy morning fog. **Best window: late morning through sunset after visibility is solid.** Relaxed bay sail or skills day; not much pressure. A front brings showers/storms Tuesday, then gustier N/NW flow Tuesday night.
- **Louisiana / Mississippi coast:** E 15–20 kt, gusts to 25, seas 3–5 ft (occasionally 6). Small Craft Advisory offshore. **No small-craft offshore plan;** protected water only with a conservative exit plan.
- **Panhandle / Big Bend:** E/NE 10–15 kt, gusts near 20, about 1–2 ft nearshore. **Best window: late morning into early afternoon, radar permitting.** Advisory conditions are posted near Apalachicola through early evening.
- **Tampa Bay / central west coast:** ENE 15–18 kt, around 2 ft; storms likely before 10 a.m. then isolated. **Best window: 10 a.m.–noon if radar clears.** Tuesday eases toward E 11–14 kt.
- **Miami / Biscayne Bay and Keys:** Bay E 10–15 kt with moderate chop and storms; exposed Atlantic around Key Biscayne E 15–17 kt with 3–4 ft (occasional 5). **Only short inshore sessions between cells; skip exposed offshore sailing.** Calmer, lighter conditions trend Wednesday–Thursday.

## Safety / tide watch

- Thunderstorms can spike wind and seas: use radar and return at first lightning/thunder.
- Re-check the exact marine zone before departure; advisories apply differently in bays and offshore.
- Galveston fog means a wait-it-out mindset, lights, traffic awareness, and AIS/radar where available.
- Check the exact NOAA tide station before departure. Easterly wind opposing an outgoing current can steepen water at Gulf passes, Biscayne Bay cuts, and inlets.
