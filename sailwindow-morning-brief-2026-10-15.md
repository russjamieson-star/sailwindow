# SailWindow Morning Brief — Thursday, October 15, 2026

**Coffee version:** Mostly light-air, light-chop sailing from Mobile through Florida; Galveston has the corridor's clearest sailing breeze at SE 10–15 kt. The key planning signal is the stronger front: it drives a Galveston Gale Watch Friday afternoon–Saturday and likely gale-force gusts across the Mobile/Pensacola area Friday night into the weekend. NHC reports no tropical cyclone formation expected in the Atlantic, Caribbean, or Gulf over the next seven days.

## Outlook and windows

- **Galveston Bay / upper Texas:** SE 10–15 kt; offshore seas 3–5 ft. Sail morning to early afternoon, then get conservative. Rain increases tonight and a Gale Watch begins Friday afternoon.
- **Mississippi Sound, Mobile & Pensacola:** Around 5 kt, turning SE/S later; waves 1 ft or less. Late-morning through mid-afternoon is a gentle practice/family-sail window. Friday night turns NW/N 20–30 kt, gusting as high as 35 kt.
- **Tampa Bay / SW Florida:** E 5–10 kt and light chop; isolated late showers/thunderstorms. Best practical window: morning through early afternoon. Northeasterlies build Saturday and become rough Sunday.
- **Biscayne Bay / Miami:** E near 10 kt and light chop, with showers/thunderstorms possible later. Best window: breakfast to early afternoon; moderate chop returns tonight.
- **Florida Keys:** Overnight easterly near 15 kt eases after sunrise, leaving a gentle-to-moderate easterly. Favor morning and keep an eye on squalls; stronger weekend conditions could bring advisories by Sunday.

## Watch-outs

- A front’s arrival timing matters more than the calm morning: check the latest zone forecast before an overnight, exposed anchorage, or Gulf crossing.
- Thunderstorms can locally spike wind and seas; leave a dock/ICW bailout route.
- The Gulf Stream remains close to the Upper Keys/Biscayne coast, so offshore routing is a different decision from sheltered-water sailing.
- Verify local NOAA CO-OPS tide and current data; tide height is not slack current.
