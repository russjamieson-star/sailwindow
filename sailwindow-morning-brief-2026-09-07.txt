Labor Day • Prepared around 7:10 a.m. EDT / 6:10 a.m. CDT

Coffee verdict: Promising for a short, flexible sail in several bays. Corpus Christi offers the clearest building breeze; much of the corridor is a light-air day. Thunderstorm timing matters more than the low wave heights. Start with your local window at https://www.sailwindow.com/.

REGIONAL OUTLOOK + BEST WINDOWS
Windows below are editorial interpretations of broad forecast periods, not hourly guarantees. Times are local. Older source issuances are explicitly marked.

• South/middle Texas — Corpus Christi, Nueces Bay and Upper Laguna Madre: S 5–10 kt, turning SE early afternoon and building late to 10–15 kt, gusts 20; increasingly choppy. Morning suits a gentler outing; afternoon suits crews seeking more breeze. Aransas/Copano bays remain near 5 kt and smooth. Farther south, the available Brownsville forecast is dated Sept. 5: its Monday projection suggests SE 5–10 kt, building late to 10–15 on Laguna Madre, but needs a fresh check before choosing a window. [1,2]

• Galveston/Matagorda — Light-air sailing, with smooth bays and roughly 5 kt; Galveston turns NW to S in the afternoon. No convincing stronger-wind window. Consider an afternoon outing only after checking storm clearance: the Sept. 6, 12:21 p.m. CDT product highlights morning–early-afternoon storms, with possible 30-kt gusts. This older issuance needs refreshing. [3]

• Southeast Louisiana/Mississippi Sound — A reasonable short-sail candidate. The Sept. 6, 5:37 p.m. CDT forecast gives Pontchartrain and western Mississippi Sound NW 5–10 kt becoming SW, waves at most 1 ft. Morning is preferable on Pontchartrain before afternoon storm chances; the Sound has only a slight storm chance in that issuance. This does not cover all western Louisiana waters. [4]

• Mobile/Pensacola/Destin — Gentle, around 5 kt, shifting northerly to southerly, with smooth water/light chop. Favor morning through late morning: rain becomes more likely in the afternoon. Northern Mobile Bay reaches 5–10 kt late, but that added breeze comes with worsening shower risk. [5]

• Panama City/Big Bend — W 5–10 kt offshore, waves at most 1 ft, but morning thunderstorms and possible waterspouts spoil a blanket “go early” recommendation. Choose a radar-confirmed break, not a clock time. St. Andrews Bay stays near 5 kt and smooth, with storms likely in the afternoon; no dependable all-clear window. Based on Sept. 6 evening issuance. [6]

• Tampa/Sarasota/Charlotte Harbor/Naples — Tampa Bay: W 5–10 kt, light chop; scattered morning storms become numerous this afternoon. Prefer a short morning gap if clear. Charlotte Harbor/Pine Island Sound: W 5–10 kt with fewer storms early, making early morning the better candidate. Naples-area waters: about 5 kt, W turning SW, seas below 2 ft; showers/storms remain possible, so no strong wind-window advantage. [7,8]

• Keys/Miami — Florida Bay and Hawk Channel: near 5 kt, smooth/at most 1-ft seas, with thunderstorm chances; no distinct best period supported by the Sept. 6, 10:33 p.m. EDT forecast. Biscayne Bay: SW near 5 kt becoming S, smooth; morning offers the better chance of a brief sail before afternoon storms become likely. [8,9]

SAFETY + TIDES
• Tropical outlook: NHC at 2 a.m. EDT today expects no tropical cyclone formation over the next seven days. That does not remove local lightning/squall risk. [10]
• Low seas do not mean storm-free water. Check local marine warnings and moving radar before departure and again before extending the trip. Big Bend morning waterspouts and upper-Texas storm gusts are specific watch-outs today. [3,6]
• Exact Sept. 7 tide predictions could not be retrieved reliably from NOAA. No high/low or inlet-slack times are claimed. Check your departure AND return station, under-keel clearance, and a separate current prediction where relevant. Tide height alone does not establish slack current. NOAA: https://tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/ ; navigation background: [12].
• For a holiday outing, allow room for wakes, traffic and a slower return. Keep the destination close enough to end the sail before threatening weather reaches you.
