# SailWindow Morning Brief — Tuesday, September 22, 2026

**Coffee read:** Yes, today is sail-able across much of the corridor, but it is mostly a light-air day. Corpus Christi has the best sustained breeze. Galveston, Mobile/Pensacola, Tampa, Biscayne and the Keys are better for easy bay sailing, drills or short hops than for fast passage-making. South Florida’s useful window closes as showers and thunderstorms build this afternoon.

## Plain-English outlook by region

- **Corpus Christi / Middle Texas Coast — best breeze:** ESE/NE 5–15 kt with 1–2 ft seas and only a slight shower/storm chance around late morning to early afternoon. **Best window: about 8–11 a.m. CDT**, with a workable afternoon if radar stays quiet.
- **Galveston Bay / Upper Texas Coast — easy, light:** N 5–10 kt becoming E, bay water around 1 ft or less, with only a slight storm chance late. **Best window: late morning through about 3 p.m. CDT** as the easterly fills.
- **Mississippi Sound / Mobile / Pensacola — flat-water skills day:** Mobile Bay runs near 5 kt, offshore early and onshore in the afternoon, smooth at 1 ft or less. Pensacola is similarly light under a north-to-south sea-breeze pattern. **Best window: noon–4 p.m. CDT** if you want the afternoon onshore puff; mornings favor calm-water practice. The Hobie 16 International Championship qualifier concludes today at Ocean Springs, with finals Wednesday–Friday.
- **Tampa Bay / Charlotte Harbor — morning only, very light:** Variable under 5 kt, 1 ft or less, with scattered storms developing after roughly 1 p.m. Tampa. Charlotte Harbor’s storm risk starts earlier, mainly after 10 a.m. **Best window: sunrise–10 a.m. EDT**, especially farther south.
- **Miami / Biscayne Bay — usable early, convection later:** SSW around 8 kt and smooth water; showers and storms become likely mainly after 2 p.m. **Best window: 8 a.m.–noon EDT.**
- **Florida Keys / Florida Bay / Hawk Channel — gentle but unsettled:** Background breeze stays light to gentle, locally moderate overnight, while unsettled showers/storms persist. The Gulf Stream edge was analyzed only 5 nm ESE of Carysfort Reef and 8 nm ESE of Molasses Reef. **Best window: morning rain gap only; verify radar and the latest short-term forecast before leaving.**

## Safety / watch-outs

- Thunderstorm gusts can be far stronger than the pleasant base wind. Turn home at the first nearby thunder, dark shelf cloud or sudden cool outflow.
- A Special Marine Warning near the Alabama coast expired overnight, but it is a reminder to check live warnings before departure.
- NHC is tracking Tropical Storm Fay far southwest of the Azores; it is not a Gulf/Florida corridor threat. One far-eastern Atlantic wave has a 30% seven-day development chance, also remote from today’s corridor sailing.
- Light wind plus heat is today’s quieter hazard: carry water, sun protection, a working VHF and enough fuel/charge to motor home.
- Check station-specific tides and inlet currents; tide height is not slack current. Mobile River’s reference prediction is high near 8:51 a.m. and low near 8:29 p.m. CDT, but local inlets will differ.
