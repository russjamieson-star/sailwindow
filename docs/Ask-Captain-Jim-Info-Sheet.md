# Ask Captain Jim — App Info Sheet

**Gulf Edition · askcaptainjim.com · Prepared for Windcraft Multihulls, October 2026**

Ask Captain Jim is not another weather app. It answers the question every skipper asks first: *is today a good day to sail, and when?* It reads live government and marine forecasts, applies your own limits and your boat type, and gives you a single score and the best departure and return times.

## At a glance

- **Works on any phone, tablet or computer.** Nothing to install: open askcaptainjim.com in a browser, or save it to the home screen like an app.
- **Covers the whole Gulf:** Texas to the Florida Keys, plus both Florida coasts.
- **7-day free trial**, then $5.99 a month or $49 a year.
- **Built for everyday sailors:** weekend and family sailors, beginners and intermediates, and new boat owners who want a clear answer rather than a pile of forecast models.

## Core features

- **Sailing Window score (0–10).** Each daylight hour is scored against your preferences. The app then finds the best block for your trip length and labels it Excellent, Good, Marginal or No-Go. Below the score, plain-English reason tags explain it, for example "Gusts 18 kt — near your limit" or "Seas 2.2 ft every 4 s — short, steep chop."
- **Best departure and return times** for the trip length you choose (1 to 12 hours).
- **Your own limits:** preferred wind range, maximum gust, trip length, tide preference (incoming, outgoing or any) and boat type.
- **Live data:** National Weather Service hourly forecasts, NOAA tide predictions from 35 verified tide stations, and the ECMWF model for the 7-day outlook, plus wave height and wave period from a commercial marine forecast service.
- **Wind and tide charts** with the best window shaded, and a high/low tide table.
- **7-Day Forecast:** a sailability score and best window for each day of the coming week.
- **Wind Map:** three days of wind, gusts and waves across the coast. Tap any spot on the water for instant readings (details below).
- **Marinas:** 80+ curated Gulf marinas with tap-to-call phone numbers, on a searchable list and map, plus nearby marinas found live wherever you are.
- **Dining ashore:** restaurants within walking distance of the marina you pull into.
- **Drawbridges:** 48 drawbridges with VHF channels, clearances and opening schedules, and the bridges near your location.
- **Cruiser's Log:** log your trips and read reports from other sailors on docking, restaurants and hazards. Every post is checked by hand before it appears.
- **Captain's Brief (rolling out now):** hand-checked news that affects boaters. Bridge and channel work, Coast Guard rule changes, marina deals, boat shows and festivals, with the most important items near you shown right under your score.
- **Weak-signal help:** if the cell signal drops, the app keeps your last forecast on screen and walks you through switching to the boat's Starlink or marina Wi-Fi.

## New for multihull sailors: Boat Type

In **Sailing Preferences**, choose **Monohull, Catamaran, Trimaran or Beach Cat**. The whole app then reads the same forecast the way your boat experiences it.

**Why it matters:** a monohull heels and spills a gust, so the skipper feels it coming. A multihull stays flat and takes the full load. For multihulls the *gap* between steady wind and gusts matters more than the average wind. And a catamaran's bridgedeck pounds in short, steep chop that a monohull shrugs off.

### Monohull (the baseline)

- Gustiness starts lowering the score once gusts run **7 kt or more** above the steady wind.
- Automatic no-go above **26 kt** of wind.
- Waves count at normal weight.

### Catamaran (cruising cats such as Seawind)

- Gustiness starts counting at a **5 kt** gust spread, and costs more per knot than for a monohull.
- Automatic no-go above **26 kt**.
- Waves count **1.5 times** as much, because short, steep chop is what makes a cat pound.
- An extra reason tag warns when it's "Gusty for a catamaran."

### Trimaran (performance tris such as Corsair and Dragonfly)

- The same stricter gust handling as a catamaran.
- Automatic no-go above **24 kt** of wind.
- Waves count **1.3 times** as much.

### Beach Cat

- The strictest gust handling: counts from a **4 kt** spread.
- Automatic no-go above **20 kt** of wind, and in seas of **4 ft or more**.
- Waves count **twice** as much.
- Choosing Beach Cat suggests gentler limits (6–15 kt wind, 18 kt maximum gust), which you can still adjust.

### Real examples (same forecast, different boats)

- **12 kt gusting 20 kt:** Monohull 8.1 · Catamaran 6.7 · Trimaran 6.7 · Beach Cat 5.3
- **3 ft seas, 4 seconds apart (short, steep chop):** Monohull 6.9 · Catamaran 6.1 · Trimaran 6.4 · Beach Cat 5.3
- **3 ft seas, 9 seconds apart (gentle swell):** Monohull 7.9 · Catamaran 7.6 · Trimaran 7.7 · Beach Cat 7.3

The two sea-state examples have the same wave height. Only the *period* differs, and the multihull scores show it.

**Note:** these limits are a careful starting point. We would welcome Windcraft's guidance on the right wind range, gust limit and "stay in" wind speed for new owners of each type, and will update the app to match.

## The Wind Map: wind, gusts and waves

Tap **Wind** in the bottom bar. The map opens on your chosen marina, at a scale that shows roughly 50 miles of coast, so you see where you are right away.

**Tap anywhere on the water for instant details.** On every layer (Wind, Gusts or Waves), one tap on any spot shows a pop-up with that exact spot's wind speed and direction, gusts, wave height and how many seconds apart the waves are. For example: "14 kt NE · gust 19" and "2.2 ft every 4 s · short, steep chop." Tap another spot to compare, or drag the time slider and the readout updates for that hour.

### Three layers

- **Wind:** steady wind speed, with moving streaks showing direction. Longer streaks mean stronger wind.
- **Gusts:** peak gusts, colored against *your* gust limit, so red means over your limit, not someone else's.
- **Waves:** wave height across the water. For multihulls this layer becomes **Sea state** (see below).

### Colors follow your boat

- Green is your preferred range. Red is a no-go for your boat type: over 26 kt for a monohull or catamaran, 24 kt for a trimaran, 20 kt for a beach cat.
- Change your limits or boat type in Sailing Preferences, and the map recolors to match.

### Sea state for multihulls

For a catamaran, trimaran or beach cat, the Waves layer shows how the sea will *feel*, not height alone:

- **Blue:** under 1 ft, flat.
- **Green:** 1–2 ft, easy.
- **Yellow:** 2–4 ft, or choppy.
- **Orange:** short, steep chop, bridgedeck slam.
- **Red:** over 6 ft, or steep seas of 4 ft or more.

Waves of 1.5 ft or more that arrive less than 5 seconds apart move up a full color, and less than 6 seconds apart move up half a color. So a stretch of water a monohull sailor sees as a moderate yellow can show orange for a catamaran. That's the point: same sea, different boat.

### How to use it

- **Tap any spot on the water, on any layer,** for instant wind, gusts, wave height and wave period at that exact spot. Check the bay, the pass and offshore in three taps before you leave the dock.
- **Press ▶** to play the next 72 hours, or drag the slider to a particular hour. Good for timing a passage or seeing when the chop lies down.
- **Jump to…** moves the map to any marina on the list.
- **The color key folds away.** Tap the key in the bottom-left corner to shrink it to its title, so it stops covering the map, and tap again to bring it back. The app remembers your choice on that device.
- **A planning tool:** wave data comes from the nearest open water, so a sheltered bay can be calmer than shown. Always check official forecasts before you sail.

## Try it in two minutes

1. Open **askcaptainjim.com** and tap **"Sailing Today? Ask Captain Jim."** The 7-day trial starts automatically.
2. Tap **Edit** on Sailing Preferences, set **Boat Type** to Catamaran or Trimaran, and save.
3. Tap **Change** on the location bar and pick **Pensacola**.
4. Read your score and the reason tags, then tap **Wind** in the bottom bar and switch to **Waves**.
5. Tap a few spots on the water, such as Pensacola Bay, the pass and offshore, and watch the readout change.
